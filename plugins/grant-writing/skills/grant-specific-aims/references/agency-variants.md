# Aims/Overview Variants by Funder

## NIH
The one-page Specific Aims format in SKILL.md is the NIH default. Under the simplified review framework (due dates on/after Jan 25, 2025), reviewers score Factor 1 (Importance: Significance + Innovation) and Factor 2 (Rigor and Feasibility: Approach) 1–9, and evaluate Factor 3 (Expertise and Resources) as sufficient/gaps. The aims page is the reviewer's first and sometimes only carefully-read page — the importance argument (Factor 1) must be complete on this page alone.

Career development (K) and fellowship (F) applications are a distinct case: the aims page interweaves research aims with the candidate's training/career-development goals, reviewers weight candidate and mentoring alongside the science, and the research must be scoped to what one trainee can execute — an R01-sized plan on a K aims page signals poor calibration.

## NSF
No "Specific Aims" document exists; the equivalent content opens the 15-page Project Description and condenses into the 1-page Project Summary (Overview / Intellectual Merit / Broader Impacts — all three headings mandatory). Frame as research objectives or questions. Broader Impacts is a scored criterion equal in weight to Intellectual Merit: education, outreach, workforce, societal benefit must appear in the overview, not as an afterthought. Include "Results from Prior NSF Support" planning if any senior person has NSF funding within 5 years.

## DoD
Structure follows the BAA/Program Announcement. CDMRP mechanisms want aims tied to the fiscal-year focus areas and an explicit military relevance statement; many require hypothesis-driven aims like NIH. DARPA-style proposals replace aims with technical objectives, tasks, and quantitative success metrics — reviewers ask "what are you going to do, how will you know it worked, why does it matter to the mission." White papers (2–5 pages) are frequently the true selection gate; give them full effort.

### CDMRP is not shaped like an NIH application — check the component list first
The single biggest trap: producing a one-page NIH-style Specific Aims page for a CDMRP mechanism that has **no such attachment**. Many CDMRP awards (e.g., the Data Science Award and similar research mechanisms) have no standalone Specific Aims document at all. Instead:

- **Aims live inside the Project Narrative** (often ~15 pages), not on a page of their own. The aims block is a section of that narrative.
- **The opening/importance and payoff work is distributed across separate one-page components** — typically an **Impact Statement**, a **Technical Abstract**, and (for translational mechanisms) a **Transition Plan** — each submitted as its own attachment and each scored. Do not fold their content into the aims block; write to each component the FOA lists.
- **Every aim must tie to the program's fiscal-year Overarching Challenges and/or Focus Areas**, and an explicit **military relevance / relevance-to-the-mission statement** is required — omitting it is a scored deficiency, not a stylistic miss.
- Prior-review architecture matters heavily on resubmissions: CDMRP summary statements often dictate how many aims and which, so reconcile the aim structure with the review before drafting (coordinate with `grant-resubmission`).

Read the specific mechanism's Program Announcement and General Application Instructions for that fiscal year to get the exact component list and page limits, and record it in `project-config.md` so the aims skill builds the right artifacts rather than assuming an NIH aims page. Component names and structures vary by program (PCRP, BCRP, PRMRP, etc.) and change year to year — verify against the current FOA. (CDMRP structure checked August 2026; always confirm against the fiscal-year announcement.)

## Foundations
Aims equivalent is usually a short objectives section in a lay-accessible narrative. Board members may not be scientists: minimize jargon, lead with the human/societal problem, keep the mechanism-of-action detail for appendices. Check whether the foundation funds "projects" vs "people" (e.g., investigator awards) — the overview emphasis shifts accordingly.
